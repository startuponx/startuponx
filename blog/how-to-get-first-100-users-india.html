How to get your first 100 users as an Indian startup founder

September 2026·8 min read

Getting your first 100 users is the hardest milestone for any startup. In India, where paid acquisition costs are rising and organic reach is competitive, the founders who win do it through direct, manual, community-driven tactics — not ads.

Here's what actually works for Indian founders getting their first 100 users.

1. Start with people you already know

Your first 10 users should come from your personal network. Not because they're your target customers — but because they'll give you honest feedback, forgive bugs, and refer you to others if the product is useful.

Message people individually on WhatsApp. Not a broadcast. A personal message: "I built something I think might help you with [specific problem]. Can I get 10 minutes of your time to show you?" This conversion rate is 10x higher than any form or landing page.

2. Post in WhatsApp and Telegram groups

India has thousands of professional WhatsApp and Telegram groups — for startup founders, developers, designers, freelancers, MBA alumni, city-specific entrepreneur communities. A genuine, non-spammy post in the right group can get you 20-50 signups in a day.

The key: don't just drop a link. Give context. "I built [product] to solve [problem] — if any of you face this, I'd love feedback from this group." That framing works. A bare link does not.

3. LinkedIn outreach — personal, specific, short

LinkedIn works for B2B Indian startups. Identify 50 people who match your ideal customer profile. Send a connection request with a short note: "Building [product] for [type of person] — would love to connect and get your perspective." No pitch. No demo request. Just connection.

After they accept, share what you're building in one sentence and ask one specific question. The conversation does the selling.

4. Build in public on X and LinkedIn

Share your building journey — what you shipped this week, what problem you're solving, what you learned. People follow builders. When you launch, they already know who you are.

Use hashtags: #buildinpublic #indiedev #indianstartup. Tag people and startups you mention. Consistency for 30 days will build a small but engaged audience.

5. List on startup directories

Startup directories are underrated for early user acquisition. The people browsing directories are actively looking for new products to try — that's high-intent traffic you can't replicate with ads.

  • Product Hunt — global launch when you're ready for volume
  • IndieHackers — bootstrapped founder community
  • Crunchbase — investor discovery and high-DA backlink

6. Reddit communities

Post in r/indianstartups, r/startups, and r/SideProject with a genuine "I built this" post. Share what problem you solved, how you built it, and what you learned. These posts regularly get 100+ upvotes and drive real traffic if the product is interesting.

Don't just drop a link. Tell the story. The story drives clicks.

7. Cold email — the right way

Find 50 people who have publicly complained about the problem your product solves — on Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit, or in startup communities. Reach out directly: "I saw your post about [problem]. I built something that might help. Can I show you for 5 minutes?"

This works because you're not interrupting — you're responding to a stated need. Conversion rates are much higher than generic cold email.

8. Offer it free to the first users

Your first 10-20 users should ideally be free or heavily discounted. Not because your product isn't worth paying for — but because getting real users using the product is worth more than the early revenue. Once you have users, you have feedback, testimonials, and word of mouth.

The pattern that works

Every Indian startup founder who reached 100 users quickly did a version of the same thing: they talked to people directly, in contexts where those people were already gathered (communities, groups, networks), and they shared something real — not a polished pitch, but an honest story about what they're building and why.

No ads. No growth hacks. Direct, personal, genuine.
